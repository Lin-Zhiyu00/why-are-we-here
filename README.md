From my perspective, code is deeply embedded in numerous aspects of daily life and hidden in countless subtle details. Communication networks we rely on, everyday tasks such as installing software or using computers for communication, and even the smallest actions—like opening a website, clicking a link, or navigating through web pages—are all closely tied to code.
Meanwhile, in a society advancing at such a rapid pace, as an art learner, I also wonder whether code can, to some extent, embody "art." The answer is undoubtedly yes. Although people often categorize code within the realm of science and engineering, clearly distinct from art, most contemporary artworks today incorporate code to create fascinating visual effects and aesthetic experiences. Take the case of Conway’s Game of Life—an AI-generated summary video highlights its essence—where code demonstrates its own kind of "life," spreading irregularly, complexly, and unpredictably. This may well answer the question: "Why are we here?" When artists master coding to a certain degree, they can express their own ideas and use it to unleash boundless creativity, generating ever-expanding designs.
The uncertainties and possibilities inherent in code-based creation align perfectly with the creative approaches artists typically employ when working with other materials; the results—unpredictable, uniquely crafted, and sometimes even surpassing all expectations—can, to some extent, challenge our conventional understanding.

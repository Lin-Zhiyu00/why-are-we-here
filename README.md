Given the rapid advancement of AI technology today, AI makes it much easier for us to learn about and apply AI – as demonstrated by my own graduation project involving a fish-shaped light installation: I needed to make the device swing at varying amplitudes. Prior to this, I had no prior experience with writing code for Arduino. During the short and hectic graduation season, attempting to learn through conventional methods and write the code myself would have been exceedingly challenging. However, AI not only helped me write the code directly but also provided valuable suggestions; whenever I wanted to make a change, it offered immediate feedback, and when an error occurred, it raised questions for me to investigate and resolve. Nevertheless, there remains one notable issue: AI has not yet evolved to be entirely error-free; it still encounters certain difficulties when dealing with technical terminology or operational aspects. This, in turn, serves as another answer to the question "Why are we here?": AI handles the production side, while I always need to understand the underlying principles, discern the quality of the output, or identify any bugs.In the future, AI may evolve to a point of being virtually invincible; in such a scenario, artists' most pure ideas would become the most valuable, yet we could still realize our creative visions in simpler or more straightforward ways.
Ultimately, I have always believed that learning to code could even help me break free from the rigid patterns of creative thinking;the sheer number of unexpected, unexplored fields and the vast creative platform—that's why I'm here, and why I am so eager to learn, and why I harbor such deep curiosity and a strong desire to explore this realm.
